Extending Motion Priors for Perceptive Quadruped Locomotion and Local Navigation

Anonymous Authors

Abstract

Current reinforcement learning (RL)-based controllers for legged locomotion significantly rely on meticulous reward tuning to track constant base velocities or goal positions while maintaining smooth motion on challenging terrains. Motion imitation methods leveraging demonstration data via RL have demonstrated the capability to achieve smooth and agile locomotion with minimal reward shaping. However, these methods exhibit limited generalization when encountering environments that did not appear in the demonstration data. To leverage the advantages of motion imitation while addressing its generalization limitations, we propose a hierarchical RL framework that integrates motion priors derived from animal motion data collected exclusively on flat terrain, followed by task-specific training aimed at goal-reaching tasks in complex environments. Our framework first pre-trains a low-level policy to imitate these references on flat terrain, forming robust motion priors. A high-level policy then leverages these priors, learning residual adjustments to concurrently manage perceptive locomotion, local obstacle avoidance, and goal-directed navigation across diverse and rugged terrain. Simulation experiments illustrate the effectiveness of learned residuals in adapting to progressively challenging terrains, achieving high navigation success rates. Furthermore, our results demonstrate improvements in energy efficiency over baseline models trained without motion priors under similar reward setups. Real-world experiments with an ANYmal-D quadruped robot confirm our policy's capability to generalize animal-like locomotion skills to complex terrains, demonstrating smooth and efficient locomotion and local navigation performance amidst challenging terrains with obstacles.
